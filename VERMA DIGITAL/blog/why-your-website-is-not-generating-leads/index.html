Why Your Website Is Not Generating Leads: 8 Common Problems

If website visits are not becoming enquiries, there may be friction in the message, experience, audience fit or measurement. Here are eight places to investigate.

29 October 2026 · Written by Verma Digital

A quiet enquiry inbox does not automatically mean that your website needs a redesign. Visitors may be arriving with the wrong expectations, struggling to use the page or simply not finding a clear reason to take the next step. Start by investigating evidence: review the page on mobile, inspect analytics and ask a few people unfamiliar with the business to explain what they understand.

Jump to a problem1. Unclear value proposition2. Poor mobile experience3. Weak calls to action4. Slow loading5. Missing trust signals6. Audience-to-page mismatch7. Complicated forms8. Incorrect conversion tracking

1. Your value proposition is unclear

Why it matters: A visitor needs to quickly understand what the business does, who it serves and why it may be a useful choice. Vague headlines and internal jargon make that decision harder.

How to identify it: Ask someone new to the business to scan the first screen for a few seconds, then describe the offer. If their answer is vague or incorrect, the page may not be communicating its value.

What to improve: Replace broad claims with a specific description of the service, audience or problem addressed. Support the headline with one useful detail and a visible next step.

2. The mobile experience is difficult

Why it matters: Small text, crowded navigation, sideways scrolling or awkward forms can prevent a visitor from completing an enquiry on the device they are using.

How to identify it: Open key pages on several real mobile screen sizes. Check whether text is readable, buttons are easy to tap, menus work and form fields remain visible while typing.

What to improve: Use responsive layouts, generous tap targets, legible type and short, clear navigation. Test complete form submission on a phone—not just the page’s appearance.

3. Calls to action are weak or confusing

Why it matters: People may be interested but unsure what happens next. Competing buttons, generic “Learn more” labels or a contact link buried in the footer create hesitation.

How to identify it: Look at each key page and ask: Is there one obvious next action? Does its label explain what happens? Can someone find it without searching the page?

What to improve: Give the primary action a clear, consistent label such as “Request a Callback” and place it near relevant information. Explain the next step and avoid presenting several equally prominent options.

4. Pages load slowly

Why it matters: A slow or visually unstable page interrupts reading and can be especially frustrating on mobile networks. Visitors may leave before the offer appears.

How to identify it: Test important pages with performance tools and on a typical phone connection. Notice large images, layout shifts, heavy scripts and delayed interactive elements.

What to improve: Resize and compress images, avoid unnecessary third-party scripts, reserve image space to prevent shifts and prioritise content needed immediately. Re-test after changes across devices.

5. Trust signals are missing

Why it matters: Before sharing contact details, a visitor needs confidence that the business is real, reachable and clear about what it offers. Unsupported claims can reduce rather than build trust.

How to identify it: Review the page as a first-time visitor. Is the business identity, contact method, service area and process visible? Do claims have appropriate context and evidence?

What to improve: Provide accurate business information, a clear explanation of your approach, transparent policies and genuine examples where permission exists. Never invent reviews, client logos, credentials or performance outcomes.

6. The audience and landing page do not match

Why it matters: Someone who clicks an ad for a particular service expects to find that service on the destination page. A generic homepage creates extra effort and may feel like a broken promise.

How to identify it: Compare campaign wording, search terms and audience needs with the landing page’s headline, offer, location and call to action. Review relevant traffic and exit patterns where tracking is reliable.

What to improve: Create or select a page that continues the promise of the referring message. Keep the service, audience and next step consistent from ad through to form submission.

7. Enquiry forms ask for too much

Why it matters: Every unnecessary field adds effort and can raise privacy concerns. Confusing validation or unclear required fields can stop someone who was ready to get in touch.

How to identify it: Complete the form yourself on mobile. Note the number of fields, unclear labels, error handling, keyboard experience and whether optional questions are truly necessary.

What to improve: Ask only for information needed to respond. Mark optional fields clearly, validate accessibly, preserve input after recoverable errors and state how enquiry information will be used.

8. Conversion tracking is missing or incorrect

Why it matters: Without reliable measurement, it is hard to know whether the site is generating enquiries or which pages and channels are contributing. A misconfigured event can make activity look healthier—or worse—than it is.

How to identify it: Submit a test enquiry and check whether the intended success event fires exactly once. Compare platform reports with actual lead records and inspect for duplicate tags or button-click events counted as leads.

What to improve: Define what counts as a meaningful enquiry, track successful completion rather than clicks alone and avoid sending names, phone numbers or other personal data to analytics. Document attribution limits.

Start with the evidence

Choose the most plausible friction point and investigate it before redesigning everything. Review the customer journey from the first message through to the completed enquiry, and prioritise changes that are useful, measurable and proportionate to your available traffic.
